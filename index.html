Part 1: The current outage

17.4 days without power. This is diabolical.

City Power's Mayfair substation caught fire in the early hours of 8 November. A second explosion the next evening, while technicians were restoring supply, knocked out the whole substation. By 12 November City Power reported the first phase of repairs 98% complete, and critical customers such as the SABC and Netcare Garden City Hospital were reconnected first. Our homes stayed without power for another 13 days.

Our power went off on 8 November 2026 and came back on 25 November 2026. That is 418.4 hours, or 17.4 days, without electricity. It went off again on 26 November 2026 and was still off when this was written.

Seventeen times the national target

NRS 047, the national quality of service standard for electricity supply, sets targets for restoring power after an unplanned outage. The longest is 24 hours. This outage ran 17.4 times that long.

Residents are already paying for their own generator

During the November outage, 133 households at Sontonga and The Exchange each paid R700 out of their own pockets to hire a generator for about 8 hours a day. That covers only the residents who could afford to contribute.

Over 8 running days it has cost R128,132: R15,826 for hire, R45,294 for delivery and installation, R60,750 for 1,937 litres of diesel, and R6,262 of hire still to be invoiced.

We had to bring the generator out twice. City Power restored supply on 16 November, the first generator was disconnected, and the supply failed again that same evening. So residents paid for delivery and installation twice: R18,478 for the first hire and R26,816 for the second.

Residents contributed R93,100, so the fund is R35,032 short. That is R963 for each paying household so far, and about R8,837 for every further day.

This is on top of the costs in the table above. Some diesel is priced at an assumed R33.75 a litre, and the second hire's delivery charge is being queried with the supplier.

And that is the conservative number

Every rand above is something a resident had to pay for just to keep living and working. The bigger costs are the ones we could not put a number on. None of these are in the R18,002,220:

Lost productivity
The hours of work and study that simply did not happen.
Lost business
The clients, contracts and income lost because the work could not be done.
Healthcare
The cost of the stress, the strain on mental health, and weeks without proper hygiene facilities.

How these estimates are worked out

The costs here are well-informed estimates. The number of households and residents, and the share who work from home, work outside the home or study, come from the residents and trustees of the three buildings. Outage dates and times come from the residents' own logs, and the generator costs are actual payments.

Only unplanned outages are counted. Scheduled load shedding is excluded from every figure.

Who lives here: 600 residents in 330 households. We estimate that 210 work from home, 150 are students and 240 work outside the home, at offices and hospitals.

A working day without power is 8 hours without power between 09:00 and 17:00 on a weekday. Public holidays are not taken out.

People who work from home: R300 for a desk in a shared office, R100 for transport there and back, and R160 each for lunch and dinner, for each working day and evening without power.

Students: R60 for transport to campus or a library and R160 each for lunch and dinner, for each working day and evening without power.

Dinner: R160 for each resident, the cost of a bought meal, for each evening the power was off at 19:00. The generator ran on some of those evenings for the households that paid for it.

Laundry: R150 for each household, one laundromat load, for each week of an outage longer than 24 hours.

Spoilt groceries: R800 for each household, for every outage longer than 24 hours.

An outage starts at the first report in the residents' WhatsApp groups and ends at the "power restored" post, so lengths are a slight undercount. Outages with no "power restored" post are left out of every figure here.

Outages before April 2025 come from the New Braamfontein Lofts residents' group, which is on a separate City Power line from The Exchange and Sontonga.

Not counted: lost income, damaged appliances, medicine that needs a fridge, security, and lost rental income.

Figures as at 26 November 2026. Prepared by the residents and trustees of The Exchange, Sontonga and New Braamfontein Lofts.
